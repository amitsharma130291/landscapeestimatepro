# Content publication standards

These requirements apply to future public pages and edits. They prevent low-value page proliferation; they do not promise rankings or a Google quality score.

1. State the user's task and the existing page that comes closest. Reuse or improve that page when a proposed addition changes only a keyword, service label, or location. A separate URL needs a materially different calculation, output, or documented workflow.
2. Record demand evidence before proposing further acquisition pages: Search Console queries, support requests, actual user interviews, or dated keyword research with source and geography. Missing evidence must be recorded as missing, never fabricated. The original five-calculator expansion was user-authorized without keyword-volume data; do not retroactively call those keywords validated.
3. Give the reader the working tool or answer before the sales pitch. Document inputs, units, formulas, rounding, assumptions, and at least one reproducible example where arithmetic is involved. Do not pad to a word-count target.
4. Label illustrative examples. Only call something a customer case study when source records exist and publication/anonymization is authorized. Never invent reviewers, credentials, testimonials, or typical financial outcomes.
5. Link to the calculation methodology and relevant next steps. Use descriptive links where they help the task; do not scatter keywords or require every page to link to every other page.
6. Keep product claims precise. Pro reuses saved inputs; users still enter scope and quantities, maintain rates, and review estimates. Do not promise that every estimate calculates itself or that a price guarantees profit.
7. Shared navigation, terminology, and truthful product facts may repeat. Review repeated editorial sections and near-identical page intent. Do not synonym-swap to evade duplication measurements.
8. Run the production build, rendered SEO audit, and relevant calculation/browser checks. For changed formulas, verify an independently calculated expected result and edge cases. Audit metrics are evidence, not a substitute for reading the page.
9. Publish modification dates only when the page actually changes. After deployment, verify URLs, canonicals, and indexation directives. Review Search Console data before deciding to merge pages that appear to overlap.
10. Report unresolved evidence gaps honestly. A passing check suite is not 100/100 content quality, independent expert review, confirmed indexing, or a guarantee of policy compliance.
